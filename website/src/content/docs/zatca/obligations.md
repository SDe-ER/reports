This page explains the main Saudi VAT rules in plain language. They are the rules the
[KSA VAT Return](/reports/reports/ksa-vat-return/) report is built on. Each rule has three parts: the rule itself,
an explanation with an example, and a link to its official source on the website of the Zakat, Tax and Customs
Authority (ZATCA). The explanation is enough to understand the rule here; the link is there for anyone who wants to
check it.

:::caution[Not legal advice]
This page summarises public ZATCA guidance as of November 2026. It is not legal or tax advice, and Sijill is not
certified by ZATCA. The Arabic versions of the ZATCA guidelines prevail, and ZATCA updates them regularly.
:::

## Registration and rate

**1. Registration is mandatory when taxable sales exceed SAR 375,000 a year.**

A business that sells taxable goods or services worth more than SAR 375,000 in twelve months must register for VAT.
After registering it gets a VAT number, and it must add VAT to its invoices and file returns.
Example: a workshop that sold SAR 400,000 of services this year must register; it is not optional.

Source: [ZATCA FAQ 024](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/FAQs/Pages/FAQ_024.aspx)

**2. Voluntary registration is allowed above SAR 187,500.**

A business with sales above SAR 187,500 but below SAR 375,000 does not have to register, but it may choose to. A
business below SAR 187,500 cannot register at all.

Source: [ZATCA FAQ 024](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/FAQs/Pages/FAQ_024.aspx),
[ZATCA e-service: VAT registration](https://www.zatca.gov.sa/en/eServices/Pages/eServices_002.aspx)

**3. The basic VAT rate is 15%.**

Most goods and services in the Kingdom carry VAT at 15% of the price before VAT. Example: a service priced at
SAR 1,000 carries SAR 150 VAT, the customer pays SAR 1,150, and the business pays the SAR 150 to ZATCA.

Source: [Guideline on the change of the basic VAT rate to 15%](https://zatca.gov.sa/ar/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/VAT15.pdf)

## Filing the return

**4. Businesses with annual sales above SAR 40 million file every month; all others every three months.**

The return is the form in which a business reports its sales, purchases and VAT for a period. Large businesses
(above SAR 40 million a year) file monthly; the rest file quarterly, four times a year.

Source: [ZATCA news, Q2 2026 returns](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/News/Pages/VAT-Returns-submission-for-Q2-June-2026.aspx)

**5. The return is due by the last day of the month after the period ends.**

When the period ends, the business has one full month to file and pay. Example: the third quarter of 2026 (July,
August, September) must be filed by 31 October 2026.

Source: [ZATCA news, Q3 2026 returns](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/News/Pages/Submit-VAT-Return-for-Oct-andQ3-2026.aspx)

**6. Filing late carries a fine of 5% to 25% of the tax.**

If the deadline passes without a return, ZATCA fines from 5% up to 25% of the tax that had to be declared.
Example: on SAR 20,000 of tax due, the fine can range from SAR 1,000 to SAR 5,000 depending on the delay.

Source: [ZATCA news, Q2 2026 returns](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/News/Pages/VAT-Returns-submission-for-Q2-June-2026.aspx)

**7. The return form has three steps: sales, then purchases, then total VAT.**

On the ZATCA portal you enter the period's sales first, then its purchases, then review the net VAT. Net VAT is the
VAT collected from customers minus the VAT paid to suppliers. If the result is positive you pay it; if it is
negative it becomes a balance in your favour.

Source: [User guide: Submit VAT Return](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/eServices/Documents/eServices_Manual_009.pdf),
[Simplified VAT filing guide](https://zatca.gov.sa/ar/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Simplified_VAT_Filing_Guidelines.pdf)

**8. Sales and purchases are entered in the return excluding VAT.**

The return boxes take the value of the sale or purchase before VAT, and the portal calculates the VAT. Example: a
sales invoice totalling SAR 1,150 is entered as SAR 1,000.

Source: [Simplified VAT filing guide](https://zatca.gov.sa/ar/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Simplified_VAT_Filing_Guidelines.pdf)

**9. Only purchases with a valid tax invoice that relate to the business are entered as recoverable.**

Not every purchase lets you recover its VAT. A purchase goes into the return only if you hold a proper tax invoice
for it and it was bought for the business. Personal purchases, purchases without an invoice, and purchases that
belong to a later period are left out.

Source: [Simplified VAT filing guide](https://zatca.gov.sa/ar/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Simplified_VAT_Filing_Guidelines.pdf)

**10. The sales adjustment box must not be used to reduce VAT without a supporting document.**

The adjustment box is for real corrections, such as a credit note issued to a customer. Sales or their VAT must not
be reduced there just to pay less: every adjustment needs an official document behind it.

Source: [Simplified VAT filing guide](https://zatca.gov.sa/ar/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Simplified_VAT_Filing_Guidelines.pdf)

## Correcting a filed return

**11. If an error in a filed return changes the VAT by more than SAR 15,000, notify ZATCA within 20 days and amend that return.**

If you discover that a return you filed was wrong, and the error changes the VAT by more than SAR 15,000 (up or
down), do not wait for the next return. Notify ZATCA within 20 days of finding the error and file an amended return
for the old period.

Source: [Guideline: Tax Invoicing and Records, section 10.4](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Guideline-for-Tax-Invoicing-and-Records-under-VAT-Provisions.pdf)

**12. If the difference is SAR 15,000 or less, correct it in the return of the period in which it was found, entering the VAT amount only.**

Small errors do not need the old return amended. Add the difference in the "corrections from previous period" box of
the current return, entering the VAT itself, not the sales or purchase value. Example: you missed SAR 900 of VAT on a
sales invoice last quarter, so you enter 900 in the corrections box this quarter.

Source: [Simplified VAT filing guide](https://zatca.gov.sa/ar/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Simplified_VAT_Filing_Guidelines.pdf),
[Guideline: Tax Invoicing and Records, section 10.4](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Guideline-for-Tax-Invoicing-and-Records-under-VAT-Provisions.pdf)

**13. A wrong return that results in less tax than due is fined 50% of the difference.**

If a return turns out to understate the VAT, ZATCA fines half of the difference, on top of the difference itself.
Example: VAT due SAR 30,000, declared SAR 20,000; the difference is SAR 10,000 and the fine SAR 5,000.

Source: [ZATCA VAT penalties](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/RulesRegulations/VAT/Pages/Penalties.aspx)

## Tax invoices

**14. A tax invoice must meet Article 53 of the VAT Implementing Regulations.**

Not every paper with an amount on it is a tax invoice. The regulations set mandatory details; an invoice missing
them is not a valid tax invoice, and the buyer cannot recover its VAT.

Source: [ZATCA FAQ 006](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/FAQs/Pages/FAQ_006.aspx)

**15. The mandatory details of a tax invoice.**

The invoice must show:
- The words "Tax Invoice".
- Its issue date and a sequential number that is never repeated.
- The supplier's name, address and VAT number.
- The customer's name and address, and VAT number if registered.
- A description of what was sold, the quantity, unit price and any discount.
- The amount before VAT, the VAT rate, the VAT amount and the total including VAT.

In practice: when you receive a supplier invoice, check that your business's name and VAT number are on it, and that
the supplier's VAT number is there.

Source: [Guideline: Tax Invoicing and Records, section 4.2](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Guideline-for-Tax-Invoicing-and-Records-under-VAT-Provisions.pdf)

**16. Invoices are issued in Arabic and in Saudi riyals.**

Invoice details must be in Arabic; another language may be added alongside. Amounts are in Saudi riyals. An invoice
without the required Arabic details is not a valid tax invoice.

Source: [Guideline: Tax Invoicing and Records, section 4.2.1](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Guideline-for-Tax-Invoicing-and-Records-under-VAT-Provisions.pdf)

**17. A tax invoice is issued within 15 days of the end of the month of the supply.**

If you delivered the service or goods in a month, you have until the 15th of the next month to issue the invoice.
Example: work done in March must be invoiced by 15 April.

Source: [Guideline: Tax Invoicing and Records, section 4.5](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Guideline-for-Tax-Invoicing-and-Records-under-VAT-Provisions.pdf)

**18. Any document by which VAT is collected is treated as a tax invoice.**

If you give a customer a document showing VAT and collect that VAT, the VAT is due to ZATCA even if the document is
not called an invoice. VAT collected from a customer cannot be kept.

Source: [Guideline: Tax Invoicing and Records, section 2 (Article 44 of the VAT Law)](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Guideline-for-Tax-Invoicing-and-Records-under-VAT-Provisions.pdf)

## Credit and debit notes

**19. A credit or debit note has the same requirements as a tax invoice and quotes the original invoice number.**

A credit note reduces an earlier invoice, for a return or a later discount; a debit note increases it. It carries
the same details as a tax invoice, states its type (credit or debit) and the number of the invoice it amends.

Source: [Guideline: Tax Invoicing and Records, section 7](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Guideline-for-Tax-Invoicing-and-Records-under-VAT-Provisions.pdf)

**20. A note is issued within 15 days of the month following the event that requires it.**

Example: a customer returned goods in May, so the credit note is due by 15 June.

Source: [Guideline: Tax Invoicing and Records, section 7.2](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Guideline-for-Tax-Invoicing-and-Records-under-VAT-Provisions.pdf)

## E-invoicing (FATOORA)

**21. Invoices are generated electronically, in a structured format, by a compliant system, stored, and carry a QR code.**

A paper or hand-written invoice is no longer enough. The invoice must come from an e-invoicing system that meets
ZATCA's requirements and carry a QR code that can be scanned to check it.

Source: [E-invoicing simplified guide](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/E-Invoicing/Introduction/Guidelines/Documents/Simplified%20guide_En.pdf)

**22. Phase 2 connects the invoicing system to ZATCA's FATOORA platform, in waves.**

In phase 2 the business's system sends each invoice to ZATCA's platform directly. ZATCA applies this to groups of
businesses (waves) by revenue, and notifies each wave at least six months ahead.

Source: [ZATCA news, Wave 25](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/News/Pages/Wave25-E-invoicing.aspx)

**23. To recover VAT on a purchase, the supplier's e-invoice must be cleared by or reported to ZATCA.**

If the supplier must be integrated with FATOORA, receiving an invoice is not enough: it must have been sent to ZATCA
as the integration phase requires before you can recover its VAT.

Source: [E-invoicing implementation resolution](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/E-Invoicing/Introduction/LawsAndRegulations/Documents/20230519_E-Invoicing%20Implementation%20Resolution%20English.pdf),
[E-invoicing regulation](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/E-Invoicing/Introduction/LawsAndRegulations/Documents/E-invoicing-Regulations.pdf)

## Recovering VAT on purchases (input VAT)

**24. VAT on a purchase is recoverable when the purchase is for the business and a KSA-registered supplier charged it correctly.**

VAT you pay to suppliers can be offset against VAT you collect from customers, on two conditions: the purchase is
for the business, and the supplier is VAT-registered and charged the correct rate. Example: spare parts for the
business's equipment from a registered supplier are recoverable; personal shopping is not.

Source: [Guideline on Input Tax Deduction, sections 4 and 5](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Guideline-on-Input-Tax-Deduction-under-VAT-Provisions.pdf)

**25. You need a valid tax invoice to recover VAT; any alternative document is at ZATCA's discretion.**

Without a proper tax invoice you cannot recover the VAT. ZATCA may sometimes accept another document proving the VAT
was paid, but that is its decision, not a guaranteed right.

Source: [Guideline on Input Tax Deduction, section 8](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Guideline-on-Input-Tax-Deduction-under-VAT-Provisions.pdf)

**26. VAT on private use, on restricted categories, or on exempt activity is not recoverable.**

Even with an invoice, VAT is not recoverable on a purchase used privately, on categories the law restricts, or on
anything that serves VAT-exempt activity. A business with both taxable and exempt activity recovers only the share
that relates to taxable activity.

Source: [Guideline on Input Tax Deduction, sections 7 and 12](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Guideline-on-Input-Tax-Deduction-under-VAT-Provisions.pdf)

**27. VAT not recovered in its period can be recovered later, within five years.**

If an invoice arrived late or you forgot to recover VAT, you can claim it in a later return, as long as no more than
five years have passed since the year of the purchase.

Source: [Guideline on Input Tax Deduction, section 9](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Guideline-on-Input-Tax-Deduction-under-VAT-Provisions.pdf)

**28. Explicit bank fees carry 15% VAT; interest and built-in margins are generally exempt.**

Fees a bank charges as a visible amount (account fees, transfer fees, card fees) carry 15% VAT. Interest and exchange
margins built into the price are generally exempt and carry no VAT.

Source: [Financial Services Sector Guideline, section 4](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Financial-Services-Sector-Guideline.pdf)

**29. VAT on bank fees is recoverable only with a tax invoice from the bank, or an alternative document ZATCA agrees to.**

The guideline does not list a bank statement as enough to recover the VAT, unless ZATCA accepts it as an alternative
document. Normally you need a tax invoice from the bank. Without one, the safe treatment is to book the whole fee,
VAT included, as a bank expense, without recovering the VAT.

Source: [Financial Services Sector Guideline, section 8.1](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Financial-Services-Sector-Guideline.pdf)

## Imports and reverse charge

**30. Services bought from a company outside the Kingdom fall under the reverse charge.**

If you buy a service from a non-resident company, such as a software subscription or server hosting, the foreign
company will not add Saudi VAT. The business then calculates the VAT itself and declares it in field 9 of the return,
and may recover it if the service is for the business.

Source: [Guideline on Imports and Exports, section 6.3](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Guideline-on-Imports-and-Exports-under-VAT-Provision.pdf)

**31. VAT on imported goods is paid at customs and can be recovered.**

When goods are imported, their VAT is paid with the customs duties, declared in field 8 of the return, and can be
recovered on the basis of the customs declaration.

Source: [Guideline on Imports and Exports, sections 4 and 10.3](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Guideline-on-Imports-and-Exports-under-VAT-Provision.pdf)

**32. Exports are zero rated, provided there are documents proving the export.**

Sales to outside the Kingdom carry 0% instead of 15%. You must keep proof that the goods actually left, such as the
customs declaration and shipping documents.

Source: [Guideline on Imports and Exports, section 7](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Guideline-on-Imports-and-Exports-under-VAT-Provision.pdf)

## Records

**33. Records are kept for at least six years from the end of the tax period.**

Invoices, books, contracts and bank statements must be kept for at least six years after the end of the period they
relate to. Capital assets have a longer period, and real estate at least 15 years.

Source: [Guideline: Tax Invoicing and Records, section 8.3.3](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/HelpCenter/guidelines/Documents/Guideline-for-Tax-Invoicing-and-Records-under-VAT-Provisions.pdf)

**34. Failing to keep invoices, books and records is fined up to SAR 50,000.**

If ZATCA asks for invoices or records and they were not kept, it can fine up to SAR 50,000.

Source: [ZATCA VAT penalties](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/RulesRegulations/VAT/Pages/Penalties.aspx)

## How the KSA VAT Return report applies these rules

**The report shows amounts excluding VAT, with the VAT in its own column** (rule 8). The Amount and Adjustment columns
hold the value before VAT, as entered on the ZATCA form, and the VAT column holds the VAT.

**The report is split into sales, then purchases, then net VAT** (rule 7). Field 13 is VAT on sales minus VAT on
purchases.

**Credit notes, debit notes and purchase returns appear in the Adjustment column** (rules 10 and 19). Only items with a
correcting document go there.

**Field 14 (corrections from previous periods) is labelled up to SAR ±15,000 and is filled by hand** (rules 11 and 12).

**Fields 8 and 9 (imports and reverse charge) are not calculated automatically** (rules 30 and 31). Complete them from
customs declarations and invoices from non-resident suppliers.

**Opening entries and transfers between VAT accounts are not shown** (rule 7), because the return covers sales and
purchases only; settling or paying VAT is neither.

**The detail report lists every document behind an amount** (rules 15 and 25): the system voucher number, the
supplier or customer invoice number, the name and the VAT number. Use it to check input VAT against tax invoices
before filing.

The report shows what is booked in the system. Whether input VAT is recoverable is decided by the rules above, so
review the [detail report](/reports/reports/ksa-vat-return-detail/) before filing.
